ACL 2025 / Modeling agents & reasoning

BPP-Search: Enhancing Tree of Thought Reasoning for Mathematical Modeling Problem Solving

Teng Wang, Wing Yin Yu, Zhenqi He, Zehua Liu, Hailei Gong, Han Wu, Xiongwei Han, Wei Shi, Ruifeng She, Fangzhou Zhu, Tao Zhong

Read the paper↗Data↗Repository↗Cite this work

BPP-Search guides tree-of-thought modeling with process rewards, beam search, and pairwise preference ranking.

01 / Research question

Tree-based reasoning offers alternative formulations, but exploring more branches is expensive and leaves another problem: choosing the correct final candidate. How can intermediate-step evaluation and final-answer selection work together to search optimization models efficiently?

02 / Approach

BPP-Search structures formulation as decisions about sets, parameters, variables, the objective, and constraints. A process reward model scores intermediate modeling steps, and beam search retains promising partial formulations. At the leaves, a pairwise preference model compares candidates that can receive similar process scores despite important modeling differences. The framework thus assigns separate roles to guiding the search and selecting its final output. The accompanying StructuredOR dataset provides explicit modeling-process annotations for training these evaluators.

03 / Research scope

StructuredOR contains 124 concrete optimization questions across domains including logistics, scheduling, and networks. In the reported search comparison, GPT-4o is evaluated on 30 solvable StructuredOR problems, 72 MAMO-ComplexLP problems, and 143 NL4OPT problems. The authors also compare different search configurations and report both correct-solution rates and reasoning steps.

Evaluation uses only test cases for which exhaustive tree traversal with GPT-4o finds at least one correct candidate: 30 StructuredOR, 72 MAMO-ComplexLP, and 143 NL4OPT problems. Steps count reasoning queries, not elapsed time.

BPP-Search improves accuracy over beam search with the same reported reasoning-step counts. This points to candidate selection as a source of gains alongside search coverage: exploring plausible paths is useful only if the system can identify the better formulation among their outputs. The comparison with larger tree traversals further motivates spending the search budget on evaluation and selection.

PRM means process reward model. BPP-Search adds pairwise preference ranking to the final candidate selection.

Adding preference ranking at the same beam width improves final selection on several benchmarks, addressing the difficulty of separating structurally similar candidates with process scores alone. Increasing the beam width helps some datasets and hurts others. Effective search therefore depends on the interaction between candidate diversity and selection quality, rather than search width alone.

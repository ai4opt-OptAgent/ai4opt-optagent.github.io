ICML 2026 / Verification & correction

Opt-Verifier: Unleashing the Power of LLMs for Optimization Modeling via Dual-Side Verification

Haoyang Liu, Jie Wang, Boxuan Niu, Xiongwei Han, Yian Xu, Mingxuan Ye, Zijie Geng, Fangzhou Zhu, Tao Zhong, Mingxuan Yuan, Jianye Hao

Read the paper↗Cite this work

Opt-Verifier improves automated optimization modeling through dual-side verification, identifying and correcting structural inconsistencies and logical errors in solutions generated by large language models.

01 / Research question

Large language models can translate natural-language problems into mathematical optimization formulations, but successful code execution does not guarantee a correct model. Generated formulations may omit implicit constraints, define variables incorrectly, or produce solutions that violate the logic of the original problem. Existing self-correction methods rely heavily on execution errors and often miss these modeling defects. This work investigates how to verify both the mathematical structure of a formulation and the contextual validity of its solutions, providing more informative feedback for model refinement.

02 / Approach

Opt-Verifier begins by extracting a three-level modeling structure from the natural-language problem: the fundamental optimization problem type, its classical variant, and problem-specific constraints. This structure guides the generation of an initial mathematical formulation. The framework then performs two complementary checks. Structure-side verification translates the generated formulation back into a structural description and compares it with the structure extracted from the original problem, identifying missing or inconsistent variables, objectives, and constraints. Solution-side verification executes the solver, interprets the numerical solution in the original problem context, and checks for logical or mathematical contradictions. Both verifiers produce judgments and actionable feedback, which a refinement agent uses to revise the formulation before producing the final solver implementation. The process extends self-correction beyond code execution errors.

03 / Research scope

The framework is evaluated on five benchmarks: NL4Opt, MAMO ComplexLP, ComplexOR, IndustryOR, and OptMATH, covering linear and mixed-integer optimization problems of varying complexity. The main implementation uses GPT-4o-mini agents and measures final performance through solving accuracy. Further experiments examine different backbone models, existing modeling systems, and the verifiers' ability to identify modeling errors.

Opt-Verifier outperforms the compared prompting methods on all five benchmarks. The results suggest that checking both formulation structure and numerical decisions against the original problem helps refinement address implicit constraints and decision logic, extending feedback beyond program execution to the meaning of the model.

Without structure augmentation: omit the extracted structure when generating the initial model and use a narrative model explanation for structure-side checking. The other two ablations disable the corresponding verification branch.

The two verification branches contribute differently across benchmarks: interpreting and checking numerical solutions is especially valuable on MAMO ComplexLP, while structural consistency has a stronger effect on IndustryOR. This pattern supports retaining both the formulation and decision perspectives. Structure augmentation also serves two purposes, guiding the initial formulation and supplying a problem-derived reference for subsequent structural checks.

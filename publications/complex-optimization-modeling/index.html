NeurIPS 2026 / Modeling agents & reasoning

Complex Optimization Modeling via Multiagent Fine-Tuning and Skill-Augmented Reasoning

Yu Ding, Jiajun Li, Ran Hou, Jiahui Duan, Guanyu Nie, Xiongwei Han, Tao Zhong, Vincent Chau, Weiwei Wu, Wanyuan Wang

Cite this work

The paper decomposes optimization modeling into six coordinated agents, creates agent-level supervision through hierarchical counterfactual sampling and solver verification, and equips the aligned system with Opt-Skill for procedural guidance and self-correction on complex OR problems.

01 / Research question

Large language models can automate simple optimization modeling tasks, but complex industrial problems combine obscure decision variables, interdependent constraints, and long reasoning chains. A multiagent workflow can divide the work, yet ordinary benchmarks reward only the final formulation or solver output and do not reveal which intermediate agent caused a failure. The paper asks how to assign reliable, sample-efficient credit to specialized agents and how to inject reusable modeling knowledge without relying on manual step-by-step annotations.

02 / Approach

The workflow assigns six agents to set identification, parameter abstraction, variable definition, objective formulation, constraint derivation, and solver-code generation. Hierarchical counterfactual sampling starts from a failed trajectory and progressively resamples the code, logical, structural, and basic layers. A solver verifier separates successful repairs from logically infeasible variants, producing verified positive and negative supervision while localizing the responsible stage. Each role is first trained with supervised fine-tuning and then refined with direct preference optimization. At inference, Opt-Skill retrieves problem-type procedures and transferable modeling patterns for the relevant agents, supporting skill-augmented reasoning, self-checking, and a refinement loop from solver feedback.

03 / Research scope

Experiments cover NL4Opt, MAMO EasyLP, MAMO ComplexLP, IndustryOR, and OptMATH, spanning relatively direct formulations and difficult industrial-style modeling tasks. The system uses Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct as its backbone and is compared with general-purpose frontier models, prompting systems such as Chain-of-Experts and OptiMUS, and fine-tuned optimization modelers including LLMOpt, ORLM, OptMATH, and SIRL. Evaluation reports solver-verified accuracy, with controlled ablations separating the effects of multiagent decomposition, SFT, DPO, and skill augmentation.

The compact 7B multiagent system reaches the best average accuracy among the reported methods. DeepSeek-V3.1 remains stronger on OptMATH, while DeepSeek-R1 ties the proposed system on IndustryOR.

The proposed system achieves 67.6% average accuracy, 2.6 points above DeepSeek-V3.1 and 7.0 points above the SIRL 7B baseline. Its largest advantages appear on the two MAMO splits, where decomposition and role specialization are especially useful for longer, tightly coupled formulations.

The multiagent prompt improves the average by 8.1 points over the single-agent prompt. After alignment, the multiagent system reaches 61.5%, and Opt-Skill adds another 6.1 points.

The controlled progression shows that decomposition, role-specific alignment, and retrieved procedural knowledge contribute cumulatively. Skill augmentation is particularly valuable on MAMO Complex and IndustryOR, where reusable modeling patterns and self-correction matter most.

Computational cost

The paper does not report a wall-clock comparison. Its efficiency claim concerns supervision: hierarchical counterfactual sampling turns solver-verified repairs into role-level positives and negatives without manual trajectory annotation, and the final system uses a compact Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct backbone.

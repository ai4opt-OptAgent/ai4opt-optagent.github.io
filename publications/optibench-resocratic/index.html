ICLR 2025 / Benchmarks & evaluation

OptiBench Meets ReSocratic: Measure and Improve LLMs for Optimization Modeling

Zhicheng Yang, Yiwei Wang, Yinya Huang, Zhijiang Guo, Wei Shi, Xiongwei Han, Liang Feng, Linqi Song, Xiaodan Liang, Jing Tang

Read the paper↗Solver library↗Cite this work

OptiBench tests end-to-end optimization problem solving; ReSocratic synthesizes structured training data from mathematical formulations.

01 / Research question

Optimization modeling requires interpreting data, constructing a formulation, and returning numerical decisions. How can a benchmark test this complete chain across nonlinear and tabular problems, and how can structured synthetic data teach smaller models to carry it out?

02 / Approach

OptiBench contains 605 problems spanning linear and nonlinear optimization, with and without tables. Models generate and execute solver code, then extract the requested variable and objective values. ReSocratic supplies training data through reverse synthesis: it expands carefully formatted seed demonstrations that pair natural-language explanations with mathematical definitions of variables, objectives, and constraints. Rule and similarity filters screen the generated demonstrations; executable code and plain-text or tabular questions are then derived from them. Intermediate demonstrations also yield step-question training pairs, producing RESOCRATIC-29K.

03 / Research scope

Evaluation separates four problem formats and reports numerical-answer accuracy alongside code execution success. A problem is solved only when all requested variable and objective values match the references. Experiments cover zero-shot and few-shot prompting, supervised fine-tuning of smaller open-source models, synthesis-component ablations, and a controlled comparison of forward and reverse data synthesis.

The category breakdown exposes bottlenecks that aggregate accuracy can conceal: handling tabular inputs and nonlinear formulations remains difficult. Fine-tuning improves performance across these formats, suggesting that structured modeling demonstrations can help smaller models connect problem interpretation with solver implementation. Keeping the category-level results makes those gains and remaining weaknesses visible.

Synthetic-data correctness is based on manual review of 30 samples per method. These 1,000-response experiments are separate from fine-tuning with the full RESOCRATIC-29K dataset.

At a fixed synthesis budget, reverse synthesis produces more accurate demonstrations and better downstream performance than the two forward methods. The comparison supports making the formulation the organizing structure of the training example: variables, objectives, and constraints are specified before the natural-language question is written. This gives the synthesis process an explicit modeling structure to preserve.

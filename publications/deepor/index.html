DeepOR: A Deep Reasoning Foundation Model for Optimization Modeling

Ziyang Xiao, Yuan Jessica Wang, Xiongwei Han, Shisi Guan, Jingyan Zhu, Jingrong Xie, Lilin Xu, Han Wu, Wing Yin Yu, Zehua Liu, Xiaojin Fu, Gang Chen, Dongxiang Zhang

Read the paper↗Cite this work

DeepOR improves natural-language optimization modeling by training a reasoning model with flowchart-guided reasoning examples and solver-informed reinforcement learning.

01 / Research question

Translating a natural-language problem into an optimization model requires identifying decision variables, objectives, and constraints, including relationships that may be implicit in the description. Existing language models often generate formulations or code directly and can miss critical modeling steps on complex problems. Although general-purpose reasoning models support multi-step reasoning, optimization-specific reasoning traces are expensive to annotate. This work investigates how to generate structured modeling traces automatically and use solver feedback to improve the accuracy of models trained for optimization formulation.

02 / Approach

DeepOR uses two training stages. First, it constructs an expert flowchart from reasoning traces generated for a small set of seed problems. Recurring reasoning steps are merged, while decision nodes represent alternative modeling paths. The flowchart then guides the synthesis of step-by-step solutions for optimization problems. These examples are used for supervised fine-tuning, teaching a base language model to reason through modeling tasks. In the second stage, the model generates formulations and executable solver code. A checklist-based evaluator combines the problem description, reference answer, generated output, and solver logs to assign rewards across feasibility, correctness, and robustness. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) uses these rewards to refine the model. When every candidate in a group is incorrect, additional flowchart-guided examples are synthesized for supplementary supervised training.

03 / Research scope

The study focuses on generating optimization formulations and solver code from natural-language descriptions. Evaluation spans seven benchmarks, from elementary linear programming to more complex industrial and academic modeling problems. The main experiments use Qwen3-8B and measure pass@1 accuracy based on the final objective value. Ablation and base-model sensitivity analyses are conducted on EasyLP and ComplexLP. The findings demonstrate improvements on these benchmarks, while objective-value agreement alone does not establish complete semantic equivalence between generated and intended formulations.

DeepOR outperforms the compared domain-fine-tuned methods on all seven benchmarks. The larger gains on complex tasks suggest that explicitly training the modeling process becomes more valuable as problems require longer chains of dependent decisions.

Expertise tuning supplies the modeling procedure that subsequent reinforcement learning refines; solver feedback is much less effective without this starting point. The flowchart-versus-linear-CoT comparison makes the structure of the training traces a central design choice: on ComplexLP, it can matter more than retaining the reinforcement-learning stage. Checklist rewards further focus refinement on fine-grained modeling quality, with greater value on harder problems.

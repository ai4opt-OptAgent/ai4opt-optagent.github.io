AAAI 2025 / Domain models & learning

Fast and Interpretable Mixed-Integer Linear Program Solving by Learning Model Reduction

Yixuan Li, Can Chen, Jiajun Li, Jiahui Duan, Xiongwei Han, Tao Zhong, Vincent Chau, Weiwei Wu, Wanyuan Wang

Read the paper↗Cite this work

The paper learns to select an interpretable reduced model - tight constraints and integer-variable values - using SetCover pruning and preference-based strategy ranking, then solves the resulting continuous problem efficiently.

01 / Research question

Directly predicting the solution of a large mixed-integer linear program creates a high-dimensional learning problem and can violate hard constraints. Model reduction offers a more structured alternative: if the integer variables and the constraints that are tight at the optimum are known, the original MILP can be reduced to a much smaller linear program. Earlier learning methods treat only the optimal reduction as correct, or regard several feasible reductions as equally useful. The paper asks how to exploit the different objective quality and feasibility of all candidate reductions, while preventing the number of strategy labels from growing with every new training instance.

02 / Approach

A reduction strategy consists of the optimal values of integer variables together with the constraints that are tight at the optimum. Applying such a strategy fixes the discrete variables and removes inactive constraints, turning the MILP into a smaller continuous linear program. The offline phase first solves sampled instances to generate candidate strategies. An instance-strategy bipartite graph connects a strategy to every instance it can solve within feasibility and suboptimality tolerances; greedy SetCover then keeps a compact subset that still covers all training instances. For learning, each retained strategy receives a reward based on its infeasibility and suboptimality on an instance. Adjacent strategies in the induced ranking provide linear-size preference samples. An attention encoder jointly represents all instance-strategy pairs, and preference plus reward-difference losses train a proxy reward model. At inference, the top-k predicted strategies are evaluated through their reduced models, and the most feasible candidate is selected.

03 / Research scope

Evaluation covers six MIPLIB scenarios, a parameterized fuel-cell energy-management problem at three scales, and five industrial inventory-management problems averaging about 100,000 constraints and reaching more than 270,000 constraints at the largest scale. Baselines include warm-started Gurobi, one-second Gurobi Heuristic, MLOPT, and Predict-and-Search where applicable. MIPLIB and inventory experiments use 1,000 training and 500 test samples; the fuel-cell study uses 10,000 training and 1,000 test samples. A solution counts as accurate when both normalized infeasibility and suboptimality are at most 1e-4.

Computational cost

The learned method performs only preference-model inference and reduced linear-system solving online. The paper reports two to four orders of magnitude speedups over Gurobi across its evaluations and about a three-order improvement overall. On the fuel-cell benchmark, the maximum online time rises only from 0.061 to 0.086 seconds as T grows from 20 to 60, while warm-started Gurobi rises from 0.379 to 33.733 seconds. The larger cost is offline: generating optimal strategies, evaluating their cross-instance performance, pruning labels, and training the preference model.
